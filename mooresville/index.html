Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Mooresville

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
