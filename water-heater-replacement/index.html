How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
