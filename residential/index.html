Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Huntersville Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing on every job, handling everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Most water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Watch for hot water with a rusty tint, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a failing dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

How old is your water heater? The date code on the label usually gives it away. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one forces the issue. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the idea behind our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we flush the sediment out of the tank, check the anode rod, test the relief valve, inspect the gas valve and venting or the elements and wiring, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all tailored to the heater in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve is ready to vent pressure if something goes wrong. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending a new heater, Huntersville Water Heater looks at the details that drive your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, back-to-back showers, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and how cold the water entering the house gets. The result is a unit with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to handle your busiest hour without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Households change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can outgrow the tank that used to be plenty. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while hybrid heat pump tanks draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Most repair calls we take come down to a handful of recurring problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element only cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and water pooling under the tank can quietly ruin flooring before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. We come prepared to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
